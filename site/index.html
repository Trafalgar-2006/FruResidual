Drosophila connectome · leaky integrate-and-fire

Play a sound into a fly's ears and listen to its wing

Johnston's organ in the antenna is the fly's ear. Fly song is a train of wing-beat pulses. Both ends are real anatomy, so audio can go in and come out the other side — carried by a spiking simulation of 165,122 real neurons wired by 25.5 million real synapses.

Listen

Pick a sound. Pick a fly.

The fly does not reproduce your sound. Its wing is not a speaker and the circuit is not a wire. What comes out is its own response, following the input's rhythm and — measurably — its pitch: in a test of eight notes the wing-motor output tracked every one to within 1%.

Male vs female

The same command, in both brains

FlyWire (female) is brain-only: pIP10, TN1, dPR1 and every wing motor neuron are absent, so song cannot be measured there at all. The one shared stage is the descending population — 1,314 male against 1,303 female. Each network is calibrated to the same population firing rate first, because the two were reconstructed by different groups and their gains differ threefold.

Read this as a null the readout caused, not as biological sameness. The mean runs over 1,314 descending neurons while the dimorphism that matters is pIP10 — 2 neurons, diluted ~650-fold. And pIP10, TN1 and dPR1 have no female counterpart at all: presence/absence, not a quantity with two values.

Why trust it

Three checks that had to pass first

Orientation

Sensory 4.0, motor 0.01. A transposed matrix leaves node count, edge count, density and E/I ratio identical while inverting every result. Only biology notices: sensory cells must send more than they receive, motor cells the reverse.

Specificity

—The song readout responds to the courtship command this many times more than to size-matched controls. Turning the gain up gives a louder signal and a worse experiment — at 0.275 it can't tell courtship from noise.

Silence where there should be silence

−0.002Hearing without the courtship command gives split-half reliability between −0.002 and +0.020 across five very different stimuli — no reproducible signal at all. With pC1 engaged it reaches +0.754.

Limits

What this does not show

  • A fly could not acoustically sing your tune. Its wing carrier is set by mechanics. The pitch shown here is what the motor neurons encode, which is a different claim from what the animal could radiate.
  • A fast surrogate was built and rejected. A 39k-parameter network imitating the simulation tracked 5 of 6 tones but collapsed on sparse and noise-like input (slow clicks r = −0.03, pink noise off threefold) and smoothed away the circuit's own rectification artefact — wrong by being nicer than what it imitates. Everything here is the real simulation.
  • Wing-motor rate is a proxy for "singing". It says nothing about song structure, the pulse and sine patterning the real circuit exists to produce.
  • No synaptic delay or neuromodulation. Transmission is instantaneous between timesteps; monoamines are set to sign zero.
  • Every number rides on one gain constant, chosen for specificity. Trust the ordering, not the percentages.
